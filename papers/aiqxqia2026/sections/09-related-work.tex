\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{$p$-adic quantum mechanics.}
Quantum systems over $p$-adic fields have a developed formalism.
\citet{AnielloEtAl2023PadicQubit} construct a model of quantum states over a quadratic
extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, working the $p$-adic qubit and quNit out explicitly.
\citet{ZunigaGalindo2024QuantumWalks} and \citet{ZunigaGalindo2025TwoAdic} study
continuous-time quantum walks whose vertex set consists of the sub-balls of a $p$-adic
ball --- that is, on the nodes of the $p$-adic tree --- and
\citet{ZunigaGalindoChaconCortes2025QuantumMarkov} relate discretised $p$-adic
Schrödinger dynamics to continuous-time quantum Markov chains. Our path states live in
$\C^{V}$ with $V$ the node set of that same tree, so the encoding of
\Cref{thm:path-states} is a state in the Hilbert space these works take as their
setting. We use it statically, as a feature map, rather than evolving it; connecting
the profile $f$ to the spectrum of a $p$-adic walk generator is a natural next question
that we do not address.

\paragraph{$p$-adic machine learning.}
The closest prior work is v-PuNNs \citep{Nguessan2025VPuNNs}, which builds neurons as
characteristic functions of $p$-adic balls, proves a finite hierarchical approximation
theorem, and reports strong results on WordNet, Gene Ontology and the NCBI taxonomy.
Our benchmarks are taken from that work so the comparison is direct. The relationship
is complementary rather than competitive: v-PuNNs is a trained architecture over
$p$-adic weight space, while this paper characterises which \emph{quantum encodings}
can carry ultrametric structure at all, and its accuracy is correspondingly higher
(\Cref{subsec:e2}). Separately, \citet{Bradley2025LocalUltrametricity} asks how
ultrametric real finite metric data actually is, recasting Vietoris--Rips complexes via
$p$-adic Mumford curves; that question is prior to ours, which assumes the target
geometry is ultrametric and asks what can represent it.

\paragraph{Quantum kernels.}
The fidelity kernel construction is due to
\citet{HavlicekEtAl2019QuantumEnhanced}, and \citet{Schuld2021KernelMethods} showed
that supervised quantum models are kernel methods, which is what makes the encoding the
locus of inductive bias. A recent line of work establishes limits on what these kernels
can do: exponential concentration as system size grows
\citep{ThanasilpEtAl2024ExponentialConcentration}, double descent
\citep{KempkesEtAl2025DoubleDescent} and benign overfitting
\citep{TomasiEtAl2025BenignOverfitting} in their generalisation behaviour, and trainable
embeddings as a response \citep{RodriguezGrasaEtAl2024NeuralQuantumKernels}. Those
results concern how a quantum kernel behaves once chosen. \Cref{thm:product-nogo,%
thm:dimension-bound} concern which kernels are reachable in the first place, and are in
that sense upstream: our obstructions hold before any question of trainability or
concentration arises. \Cref{thm:dimension-bound} in particular does not care whether
the encoding concentrates, only how wide it is.

\paragraph{Hierarchy in classical representation learning.}
Hyperbolic embeddings are the established answer to representing trees
\citep{NickelKiela2017Poincare}, and the approach has grown into a substantial
literature \citep{HeEtAl2025HyperbolicSurvey}. Hyperbolic space achieves low but
non-zero distortion of tree metrics, because a tree embeds into it only approximately.
The path-state construction achieves zero distortion by design, at the cost of a
dimension linear rather than logarithmic in the number of nodes --- and, by
\Cref{thm:dimension-bound}, that cost is not an artefact of our construction but a
lower bound for any exact realisation.

\paragraph{Data re-uploading.}
Interleaving data encodings with trainable layers is due to
\citet{PerezSalinasEtAl2020DataReuploading} and remains actively developed
\citep{CasseEtAl2024QuantumReuploading}. We use it in \Cref{sec:reuploading} not to
increase expressivity for its own sake but as the minimal way to extend a fixed
encoding at all, given the invariance \cref{eq:unitary-invariance}.
