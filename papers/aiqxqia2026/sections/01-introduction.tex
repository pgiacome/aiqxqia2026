\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A quantum kernel method embeds classical data through a feature map $\Phi$ and compares
two points by the fidelity of their images,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:fidelity-kernel}
  K(x, y) = \fid{\Phi(x)}{\Phi(y)} .
\end{equation}
Everything a downstream model can express about the similarity of $x$ and $y$ passes
through this one number, and the number is fixed by the encoding alone. Whatever
geometry $\Phi$ imposes on the data is imposed before any parameter is trained
\citep{Schuld2021KernelMethods,HavlicekEtAl2019QuantumEnhanced}. The choice of encoding
is therefore not a preprocessing detail. It is the model's inductive bias.

Much of the data to which quantum machine learning is applied is hierarchical.
Taxonomies, ontologies, phylogenies, file systems and type lattices are trees, and the
natural notion of distance on a tree is not Euclidean but \emph{ultrametric}: distances
obey the strong triangle inequality
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:strong-triangle}
  d(x, z) \le \max\bigl(d(x, y),\, d(y, z)\bigr),
\end{equation}
which is strictly stronger than the ordinary triangle inequality. Equivalently, the
points sit at the leaves of a tree and distance decreases with the depth of the lowest
common ancestor. The canonical ultrametric space is the ring of $p$-adic integers
$\Zp$, whose valuation $\vp(x - y)$ counts matching least-significant base-$p$ digits
and so measures exactly that depth.

This paper asks one question.

\begin{quote}
  \emph{Which quantum feature maps induce an exactly ultrametric kernel?}
\end{quote}

We answer with two obstructions and a matching construction.

\paragraph{Theorem A: product encodings cannot do it.}
A feature map that factorises across digits induces a multiplicative kernel, and
multiplicativity bounds how deep such a map can resolve the tree, whatever the local
dimension: a product map is left with three kernel values, too few for a strictly
monotone profile once $n \ge 2$. The argument generalises from single digits to disjoint
blocks, where the resolution bound is set by the size of one block, so it constrains a
class of encodings rather than a list of them. Angle encoding and computational-basis
encoding are corollaries.

\paragraph{Theorem B: narrow encodings cannot do it either, whatever their structure.}
The Gram matrix of any exact realisation is positive semi-definite with unit diagonal,
which forces its rank above $L^{2} / \sum_{x,y} K(x,y)$ on $L$ points. On the regular
$p$-ary tree with a geometrically decaying profile of parameter $s > 1$, this exceeds
$2^{n}$ for every $p \ge 3$ once $n$ passes an explicit threshold: beyond it, \emph{no}
$n$-qubit encoding realises that kernel on $\Zpn$, entangling or not. This is what rules
out IQP and ZZ feature maps, which Theorem~A does not reach.

\paragraph{Theorem C: path states do it exactly, at essentially the optimal width.}
Writing $\anc_i(x)$ for the depth-$i$ ancestor of a leaf $x$, the state
$\Phi_f(x) = \sum_i a_i \ket{\anc_i(x)}$ realises \emph{every} strictly monotone
ultrametric kernel exactly, with amplitudes determined by the target profile. The
construction needs $\lceil \log_2 \abs{V} \rceil$ qubits for a tree on $\abs{V}$ nodes,
$n+1$ non-zero amplitudes, and $O(n \log \abs{V})$ gates, and it matches Theorem~B to
within an additive constant.

Along the way we record a fourth fact with a two-line proof. Global depolarising noise
acts on the induced distance as a non-decreasing affine map, so it \emph{cannot} break
the strong triangle inequality or level constancy at any error rate
(\Cref{prop:depolarising}). What it destroys is contrast rather than geometry. Finite
sampling behaves in the opposite way and breaks the strong triangle inequality
immediately, for a reason specific to ultrametric spaces that we make precise in
\Cref{sec:experiments}.

\subsection*{What this paper does not claim}

The kernel of \Cref{thm:path-states} is $K(x,y) = f(\lcadepth(x,y))$, and the lowest
common ancestor of two leaves is computable classically in time linear in the depth of
the tree. \textbf{There is no quantum speedup in kernel evaluation here, and we claim
none.} We state this in the introduction rather than leaving a reader to discover it,
because it determines how the rest should be read. The contribution is threefold:
a no-go that constrains current encoding practice; a dimension bound that applies to
arbitrary encodings and so survives future encoding designs; and an explicit,
optimal-width encoding usable as the input stage of deeper quantum models, where no
closed form survives the subsequent layers.

\subsection*{Contributions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item[\textbf{C1}] A no-go theorem for product feature maps
    (\Cref{thm:product-nogo}), which bounds the resolution depth of any block-product
    map by the size of one block and collapses to three kernel values for genuine
    product maps, with corollaries for angle and computational-basis encodings.
  \item[\textbf{C2}] A dimension lower bound for \emph{arbitrary} feature maps
    (\Cref{thm:dimension-bound}), which rules out every $n$-qubit encoding of the
    geometric profile on $\Zpn$ for $p \ge 3$ and $n$ past an explicit threshold, and
    so covers the entangling maps Theorem~A cannot reach.
  \item[\textbf{C3}] An exact construction (\Cref{thm:path-states}) realising precisely
    the strictly monotone ultrametric kernels, with a resource count matching C2 to
    within an additive constant, and a converse characterising every realisation with
    non-negative overlaps up to isometry and phase.
  \item[\textbf{C4}] A generalisation beyond the homogeneous case: the construction
    needs only that ancestors nest, so it applies to arbitrary rooted trees, with
    $\Zpn$ as the specialisation in which the profile ties to $\abs{\cdot}_p$.
  \item[\textbf{C5}] An empirical study on WordNet, Gene Ontology and the NCBI taxonomy
    separating ultrametricity from usable resolution, mapping the
    expressivity/hierarchy trade-off of a re-uploading extension, and quantifying the
    shot budget the construction needs.
\end{itemize}
