\label{sec:preliminaries}

\subsection{$p$-adic integers and the regular tree}

Fix a prime $p$ and an integer $n \ge 1$. Write $X = \Zpn$ and expand each $x \in X$ in
base $p$ as $x = \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i p^{\,i-1}$ with digits $x_i \in \{0, \dots, p-1\}$.
The $p$-adic valuation of a non-zero integer is the exponent of the largest power of
$p$ dividing it, and the $p$-adic absolute value is $\abs{m}_p = p^{-\vp(m)}$. On $X$
we use the truncated valuation
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:valuation}
  v(x, y) = \min\bigl(\vp(x - y),\, n\bigr)
  = \#\{\text{matching least-significant base-$p$ digits of $x$ and $y$}\} .
\end{equation}

Identify $X$ with the leaves of the regular $p$-ary tree of depth $n$, a leaf $x$
sitting under the chain of residue classes $x \bmod p, \, x \bmod p^{2}, \dots$. Then
the depth-$i$ ancestor of $x$ is the class $x \bmod p^{i}$, and $v(x,y)$ is exactly the
depth of the lowest common ancestor of $x$ and $y$.

\begin{example}
  \label{ex:padic-tree}
  Take $p = 3$, $n = 2$, so $X = \{0, \dots, 8\}$. The leaves $x = 2$ and $y = 5$ have
  $x - y = -3$, hence $\vp(x-y) = 1$: they agree in the least-significant digit
  ($2 \equiv 5 \bmod 3$) and differ in the next, so they meet at depth $1$. The leaves
  $2$ and $4$ differ already in the least-significant digit and meet only at the root,
  at depth~$0$. \Cref{fig:padic-tree} draws the tree.
\end{example}

\subsection{Ultrametric spaces}

A metric $d$ on a set $X$ is an \emph{ultrametric} if it satisfies
\cref{eq:strong-triangle} for all $x, y, z$. Two consequences matter here. First, every
triangle is isoceles with its two longest sides equal, so a constant fraction of
triples meets \cref{eq:strong-triangle} \emph{with equality}. That fact turns out
to govern how ultrametricity behaves under sampling noise
(\Cref{sec:experiments}). Second, the closed balls of an ultrametric space are nested
or disjoint, never partially overlapping, which is precisely the combinatorics of a
rooted tree.

Real hierarchical data is rarely exactly ultrametric, and quantifying the discrepancy
is an active question \citep{Bradley2025LocalUltrametricity}. Our concern is the
complementary one: given that the target geometry \emph{is} ultrametric, which
encodings can represent it without distortion.

\subsection{Quantum feature maps and fidelity kernels}

A quantum feature map is a function $\Phi : X \to \C^{D}$ with $\norm{\Phi(x)} = 1$,
realised in practice by a state-preparation circuit on $\lceil \log_2 D \rceil$ qubits.
Its fidelity kernel is \cref{eq:fidelity-kernel}. Writing
$G_{xy} = \braket{\Phi(x)}{\Phi(y)}$ for the Gram matrix of the feature vectors, we
have $K = \abs{G}^{\circ 2}$ entrywise, and $G$ is positive semi-definite with
$G_{xx} = 1$. Since the Schur product of positive semi-definite matrices is positive
semi-definite, $K$ is a valid kernel for any $\Phi$.

Two families of encoding recur below. An \emph{angle encoding} assigns one qubit per
digit, $\ket{\psi_i(a)} = \cos(\theta_a/2)\ket{0} + \sin(\theta_a/2)\ket{1}$, and takes
the tensor product. A \emph{ZZ (or IQP-style) feature map} applies a data-dependent
diagonal phase between layers of Hadamards,
$\Phi(x) = \bigl(U_{\phi(x)} H^{\otimes n}\bigr)^{r}\ket{0}^{\otimes n}$, with the
phase carrying both single-qubit and pairwise terms
\citep{HavlicekEtAl2019QuantumEnhanced}. The pairwise terms make the ZZ map entangling,
which is exactly why \Cref{thm:product-nogo} does not apply to it and
\Cref{thm:dimension-bound} must.
