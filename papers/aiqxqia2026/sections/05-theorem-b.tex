\section{A Dimension Lower Bound for Arbitrary Encodings}
\label{sec:theorem-b}

\Cref{thm:product-nogo} exploits the structure of the encoding. The bound in this
section uses none: it constrains any map whatsoever into a space of given dimension,
and so applies to entangling encodings, hardware-efficient ansätze, and designs not yet
proposed.

\begin{theorem}[Dimension lower bound]
  \label{thm:dimension-bound}
  Let $\Phi : X \to \C^{D}$ send $L$ points to unit vectors with
  $\fid{\Phi(x)}{\Phi(y)} = K(x,y)$. Then
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:dimension-bound}
    D \;\ge\; \rank G \;\ge\; \frac{L^{2}}{\sum_{x,y} K(x,y)} ,
  \end{equation}
  where $G$ is the Gram matrix of the $\Phi(x)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The vectors $\Phi(x)$ lie in $\C^{D}$, so $\rank G \le D$. Let $r = \rank G$ and let
  $\mu_1, \dots, \mu_r$ be the non-zero eigenvalues of $G$, which are real and
  non-negative because $G$ is positive semi-definite. Since $\norm{\Phi(x)} = 1$ we
  have $G_{xx} = 1$ and hence $\tr G = \sum_k \mu_k = L$. By Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
  the vector of eigenvalues,
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:frobenius}
    \norm{G}_F^{2} = \sum_{k=1}^{r} \mu_k^{2}
    \;\ge\; \frac{1}{r}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{r} \mu_k\Bigr)^{2} = \frac{L^{2}}{r} .
  \end{equation}
  Finally $\norm{G}_F^{2} = \sum_{x,y} \abs{G_{xy}}^{2} = \sum_{x,y} K(x,y)$ by the
  definition of the fidelity kernel. Rearranging \cref{eq:frobenius} gives
  \cref{eq:dimension-bound}.
\end{proof}

The bound is a statement about how \emph{concentrated} the kernel is. A kernel close to
the all-ones matrix has $\sum_{x,y} K \approx L^{2}$ and demands only one dimension,
correctly: a near-constant kernel is realisable by a single state. A kernel close to
the identity has $\sum_{x,y} K \approx L$ and demands $L$ dimensions, also correctly.
Strictly monotone ultrametric profiles decay fast enough to sit near the second regime,
and the following corollary makes that quantitative.

\begin{corollary}[No $n$-qubit encoding suffices for $p \ge 3$]
  \label{cor:no-n-qubit}
  On the regular $p$-ary tree of depth $n$ with the geometric profile
  $f(v) = p^{-(n-v)s}$, put
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:row-sum}
    S(p, s, n) \;=\; 1 + \frac{p-1}{p} \sum_{m=1}^{n} p^{\,m(1-s)} .
  \end{equation}
  Then any exact realisation needs $D \ge p^{n} / S(p,s,n)$, that is, at least
  $n \log_2 p - \log_2 S(p,s,n)$ qubits. For $s > 1$ the sum converges and
  $S(p,s,n) \le S(p,s) < \infty$ uniformly in $n$, so the qubit requirement grows like
  $n \log_2 p$. For every $p \ge 3$ it exceeds $n$, and hence \emph{no $n$-qubit
  encoding of any kind realises a strictly monotone ultrametric kernel on $\Zpn$}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Fix a leaf $x$. The number of leaves $y$ with $\lcadepth(x,y) = v$ is
  $p^{\,n-v} - p^{\,n-v-1}$ for $v < n$, and $1$ for $v = n$. Hence
  $\sum_{y} f(\lcadepth(x,y)) = S(p,s,n)$ after substituting $m = n - v$, and by
  symmetry $\sum_{x,y} K = p^{n} S(p,s,n)$. Inserting this and $L = p^{n}$ into
  \cref{eq:dimension-bound} gives the bound. For $s > 1$ the geometric series
  $\sum_m p^{\,m(1-s)}$ converges, so $S$ is bounded uniformly in $n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}
  \label{ex:crossing}
  At $p = 3$, $s = 2$ we get $S = 4/3$ in the limit, so the requirement is
  $q \ge n \log_2 3 - \log_2(4/3) \approx 1.585\,n - 0.415$, above $n$ for every
  $n \ge 1$. Concretely, at $n = 5$ the bound is $\QubitBoundPThree$ qubits against the
  $5$ that an angle or ZZ encoding provides, and at $p = 5$ it is $\QubitBoundPFive$.
  \Cref{fig:e3-ablation}(b) plots the crossing.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[The hypothesis $s > 1$ is load-bearing]
  \label{rem:s-greater-one}
  At $s = 1$ every term of \cref{eq:row-sum} equals $1$ and
  $S(p,1,n) = 1 + n(p-1)/p$ grows with the depth, so the bound degrades to
  $p^{n} / \Theta(n)$ and \Cref{cor:no-n-qubit} no longer follows from it. This is not
  an artefact of the proof: a slowly decaying profile really is easier to represent,
  because it is closer to constant. We record the boundary rather than quietly assuming
  it away (\texttt{test\_theorem\_b\_requires\_s\_greater\_than\_one}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Theorems A and B are independent]
  \label{rem:complementarity}
  At $p = 2$ the bound reads $q \ge n - \log_2 S(2,s) \approx n - 0.58$, which does
  \emph{not} exceed $n$. \Cref{thm:dimension-bound} therefore leaves $n$-qubit
  encodings on binary trees open, and it is \Cref{thm:product-nogo}'s structural
  argument that closes the product case there. Conversely \Cref{thm:product-nogo} says
  nothing about entangling maps at any radix. Neither theorem subsumes the other, and
  we present them as covering different regimes rather than as a chain
  (\texttt{test\_theorem\_b\_does\_not\_subsume\_theorem\_a\_at\_radix\_two}).
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Numerical verification.}
The closed form \cref{eq:row-sum} matches the empirical row sum to machine precision
(\texttt{test\_theorem\_b\_closed\_form\_row\_sum}); the bound exceeds $2^{n}$ for
$(p,n) \in \{(3,2),(3,3),(3,4),(5,2)\}$
(\texttt{test\_theorem\_b\_rules\_out\_every\_n\_qubit\_encoding}); and the ZZ feature
map, which lives in $2^{n}$ dimensions, falls below the bound and indeed fails to
reproduce the profile
(\texttt{test\_theorem\_b\_zz\_feature\_map\_is\_below\_the\_bound}).

Two cautions on applying \cref{eq:dimension-bound} in practice. Evaluated on a
subsample of $L'$ leaves it remains valid --- restricting the point set can only make
the kernel easier to realise --- but it is capped at $L'$, so a subsampled estimate
understates the bound and saturates at $\log_2 L'$ qubits. The figures in
\Cref{sec:experiments} therefore use the closed form. And the bound constrains
dimension, not circuit depth: it says nothing about how hard the required state is to
prepare, which is the subject of \Cref{sec:theorem-c}.
