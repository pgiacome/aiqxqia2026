\section{Ultrametric Quantum Kernels}
\label{sec:problem}

Throughout, $T$ is a finite rooted tree whose leaves all sit at depth $n$ (a leaf that
is shallower is extended by a chain of unary nodes, which changes no lowest common
ancestor). For a leaf $x$ and $0 \le i \le n$ we write $\anc_i(x)$ for its depth-$i$
ancestor, so $\anc_0(x)$ is the root and $\anc_n(x) = x$, and
$\lcadepth(x, y) = \max\{\, i : \anc_i(x) = \anc_i(y) \,\}$ for the depth at which two
leaves meet. On the regular $p$-ary tree, $\lcadepth = v$ of \cref{eq:valuation}.

\begin{definition}[Ultrametric kernel]
  \label{def:ultrametric-kernel}
  A kernel $K : X \times X \to [0,1]$ is \emph{ultrametric with profile $f$} if there
  is a function $f : \{0, \dots, n\} \to [0,1]$ with $f(n) = 1$ such that
  $K(x, y) = f\bigl(\lcadepth(x,y)\bigr)$ for all leaves $x, y$. The profile is
  \emph{strictly monotone} if $f$ is strictly increasing and $f(0) > 0$.
\end{definition}

Being ultrametric with \emph{some} profile is the same as being constant on each level
of the lowest-common-ancestor relation, a property we call \emph{level constancy}.
Level constancy alone is not enough to make the induced distance an ultrametric:
$f = (0.9, 0.1, 1)$ on a depth-$2$ tree is level-constant, yet two leaves meeting at
depth $1$ are further apart than either is from a leaf meeting them at the root.
Monotonicity is what supplies the strong triangle inequality.

\begin{lemma}
  \label{lem:distance-is-ultrametric}
  If $K$ is ultrametric with a non-decreasing profile $f$ satisfying $f(v) < 1$ for
  every $v < n$, then $d = 1 - K$ is an ultrametric on the leaves of $T$. Without the
  second hypothesis $d$ is a pseudo-ultrametric: $f \equiv 1$ gives $d \equiv 0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Lowest-common-ancestor depth satisfies
  $\lcadepth(x,z) \ge \min\bigl(\lcadepth(x,y), \lcadepth(y,z)\bigr)$, because the
  ancestors of a leaf form a chain: if $x$ and $y$ agree down to depth $i$ and $y$ and
  $z$ agree down to depth $i$, then $x$ and $z$ agree down to depth $i$. Applying the
  non-decreasing $f$ preserves this and reverses it under $d = 1 - f$, giving
  \cref{eq:strong-triangle}. Non-degeneracy follows from $f(n) = 1$ and $f(v) < 1$ for
  $v < n$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Two diagnostics, because one is not enough}
\label{subsec:diagnostics}

It is tempting to certify an encoding by counting violations of
\cref{eq:strong-triangle}. That test is necessary but far from sufficient, and the gap
is not a technicality. Computational-basis encoding sends distinct leaves to orthogonal
states, so its kernel is the identity and its induced distance is the discrete metric
--- which is an ultrametric, with zero violations, while collapsing the hierarchy to a
single bit of information. It is the degenerate corner of
\Cref{def:ultrametric-kernel}, with $f = \mathbf{1}[v = n]$.

We therefore report two quantities throughout.

\begin{definition}[Level constancy and resolution depth]
  \label{def:diagnostics}
  Call a level $v$ \emph{populated} if some pair of leaves has $\lcadepth(x,y) = v$,
  and let $V(K)$ collect them. The \emph{level constancy} is
  \begin{equation*}
    \max_{v \in V(K)} \bigl( \max K_v - \min K_v \bigr),
    \qquad K_v = \{\, K(x,y) : \lcadepth(x,y) = v \,\},
  \end{equation*}
  which vanishes exactly when $K$ is ultrametric with some profile. Writing
  $\mu_v$ for the mean of $K$ over level $v$, the \emph{resolution depth} is the
  number of runs in the sequence $(\mu_v)_{v \in V(K)}$ after merging consecutive
  entries differing by at most $\tau$; we take $\tau = 10^{-6}$ throughout, a scale no
  realistic shot budget could resolve (\Cref{subsec:e5}). It equals $\abs{V(K)}$ for a
  strictly monotone profile and $2$ for the discrete metric.

  Restricting to populated levels matters on padded and irregular trees, where some $v$
  may carry no pair of leaves at all. At such a $v$ the profile is not determined by
  $K$, so \Cref{thm:path-states} should be read as a bijection onto profiles restricted
  to $V(K)$.
\end{definition}

An encoding is useful for hierarchical data only if it scores well on both: level
constancy zero, resolution depth full. \Cref{thm:path-states} exhibits the only family
that does.

\subsection{Product and block-product feature maps}

\begin{definition}[Product feature map]
  \label{def:product-map}
  A feature map on $\Zpn$ is a \emph{product map} if
  $\Phi(x) = \bigotimes_{i=1}^{n} \ket{\psi_i(x_i)}$ for unit vectors
  $\ket{\psi_i(a)} \in \C^{d_i}$, where factor $i$ depends only on digit $i$ and the
  local dimensions $d_i$ are arbitrary. Writing
  $g_i(a,b) = \fid{\psi_i(a)}{\psi_i(b)}$, we have $g_i(a,a) = 1$ and
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:multiplicative}
    K(x, y) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} g_i(x_i, y_i).
  \end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Block-product feature map]
  \label{def:block-product-map}
  Let $\mathcal{B}$ be a partition of the levels $\{1, \dots, n\}$ into consecutive
  blocks. A \emph{block-product map} is
  $\Phi(x) = \bigotimes_{B \in \mathcal{B}} \ket{\psi_B(x_B)}$, where $x_B$ is the
  tuple of digits in block $B$. Its kernel factorises across blocks in the same way.
  A product map is the case in which every block is a single level.
\end{definition}

Block-product maps let a bounded number of levels interact arbitrarily while keeping
the tensor structure across blocks. They are the natural strengthening of
\Cref{def:product-map}, and \Cref{thm:product-nogo} handles both at once. They do not,
however, cover maps such as the ZZ feature map, whose all-pairs couplings leave no
tensor factorisation at all; that case is \Cref{sec:theorem-b}.
