\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

All experiments are analytic or statevector simulations on CPU, each completing in
seconds to minutes. Seeds, resolved configurations and library versions are recorded
with every run, and every number below is generated from the experiment outputs rather
than transcribed.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

We use WordNet nouns via NLTK, the molecular-function namespace of the Gene Ontology,
the Mammalia clade of the NCBI taxonomy, and synthetic regular $p$-ary trees for
controlled ablations. \Cref{tab:datasets} gives the shapes.

\paragraph{Preprocessing, stated rather than assumed.}
Each source is a directed acyclic graph in the wild, not a tree: WordNet synsets can
have several hypernyms. We reduce each to a tree by keeping, for every node, the
\emph{longest} path to the root, breaking ties by lexicographically smallest identifier.
Multiple inheritance is therefore discarded, which \Cref{sec:limitations} records as a
limitation. We then truncate every hierarchy at depth $\EOneHeight$, because the
$2^{\text{height}}$-dimensional product baselines are otherwise not representable:
WordNet reaches depth $\EOneSourceHeight$, so an angle or ZZ encoding would need
$2^{\EOneSourceHeight}$ amplitudes per point. Truncation also puts every encoding on the
same tree. Finally we sample $\EOneLeaves$ leaves, restrict to their ancestor closure,
and pad shallow leaves to uniform depth. None of these steps changes the
lowest-common-ancestor depths among the sampled leaves below the truncation, which is
all the kernel depends on.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Angle encoding, computational-basis encoding, a ZZ/IQP feature map with two repetitions,
a random product map, a block-product map where representable, and two classical
references: RBF kernels on integer leaf coordinates and on a one-hot encoding. The
geometric profile uses base $2$ for the real datasets. Tying the base to the branching
factor instead, $\NCBIRadix$ for NCBI, pushes $f(0)$ to around $10^{-13}$, below any
tolerance a realistic shot budget could support, so the base is treated as the free
design parameter it is.

\Cref{tab:e1} reports both diagnostics of \Cref{def:diagnostics} on the three real
hierarchies; synthetic trees get a systematic sweep in \Cref{subsec:e3}. The path-state encoding attains level constancy $\PathLevelConstancy$ and
zero violations everywhere, at full resolution: the construction is exact on real
hierarchies, not only on the regular trees of \Cref{thm:path-states}. Every product and
entangling baseline has level constancy near $1$, angle reaching
$\AngleLevelConstancy$ and the random product map $\RandProdLevelConstancy$, so their
kernels are not imperfect ultrametrics but not ultrametric at all, as
\Cref{thm:product-nogo,thm:dimension-bound} predict.

Worth stating plainly, because it complicates the headline: \textbf{basis encoding also
achieves zero violations}, with level constancy exactly zero and resolution
$\BasisResolution$ out of $\EOneLevels$. This is the $v^{*} = n$ corner discussed in
\Cref{sec:theorem-a}, and it is why we do not claim the path state is the unique
encoding with an ultrametric kernel. The correct claim is narrower: it is the only one
that is both ultrametric and fully resolving.

\subsection{E2: hierarchical classification}
\label{subsec:e2}

    $C = 1$; no hyperparameter was tuned. A dash marks an undefined rank correlation: a
    delta kernel gives every distinct pair the same distance.}
  \label{tab:e2}
  \footnotesize
  \input{tables/e2-accuracy}
\end{table}

\Cref{tab:e2} predicts a leaf's depth-$2$ ancestor and correlates the kernel's induced
distance with true tree distance. Classes with fewer than five members are dropped, with
the count recorded in the run metadata.

The path state attains $\rho = \ETwoPathRho$ on every dataset. This is a consequence of
\cref{eq:exactness}, not a fitted result: the induced distance is a strictly monotone
function of tree distance, so perfect rank correlation follows. No baseline exceeds
$\ETwoBestBaselineRho$.

On accuracy the picture is different, and we report it as it came out. On WordNet the
path state reaches $\ETwoPathAccuracy \pm \ETwoPathAccuracyStd$ while the
\ETwoBestBaseline{} baseline reaches $\ETwoBestBaselineAccuracy$, with a similar
ordering on Gene Ontology. A kernel that faithfully encodes tree distance is therefore
not the best kernel for this task, which is unsurprising once stated: the label is the
depth-$2$ ancestor, and a kernel spending capacity on depths $3$ through $\EOneHeight$
spends it on distinctions the label ignores. The comparison against v-PuNNs
\citep{Nguessan2025VPuNNs}, at $99.96\%$ leaf accuracy on WordNet, is not close and must
be read the same way: that is a trained deep model with a bespoke optimiser over
$p$-adic weight space, this a fixed encoding with a closed form and no parameters.
Geometric fidelity and task accuracy are different objectives; this paper claims the
former. The criterion was fixed before any number was produced, so that a weak result
could not tempt a retrofit of the claims.

\subsection{E3 and E4: ablations and the re-uploading trade-off}
\label{subsec:e3}

\begin{figure}
  \centering

    the $n$ qubits a product or IQP encoding provides: above the diagonal for
    $p \ge 3$, below it for $p = 2$. (c) Profile choice against accuracy.}
  \label{fig:e3-ablation}
\end{figure}

\Cref{fig:e3-ablation} sweeps profile shape, depth and branching factor over
$\EThreeConfigs$ configurations. Panel~(a) is the robustness check for
\Cref{thm:path-states}: the worst residual is $\EThreeWorstResidual$ and the total
violation count is $\EThreeMaxViolations$, so exactness degrades with neither depth,
radix nor profile shape. Panel~(b) is \Cref{cor:no-n-qubit} in picture form. For $p = 3$
and $p = 5$ the required qubit count runs above the diagonal at every depth; for
$p = 2$ it runs below, which is \Cref{rem:complementarity}. The curves use the closed
form \cref{eq:row-sum} on the full tree, since a subsampled estimate saturates at
$\log_2$ of the sample size.

\begin{figure}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.78\linewidth]{figures/e4_pareto}
  \caption{E4 on WordNet, over $\EFourConfigs$ configurations of \cref{eq:reupload}.
    Distortion grows continuously from zero, so the exact construction is the endpoint
    of a tunable family rather than an isolated point; the alignment bought in exchange
    is small.}
  \label{fig:e4-pareto}
\end{figure}

E4 answers the structural question about \cref{eq:reupload} affirmatively: distortion
grows continuously from zero, so the exact construction is the zero-distortion endpoint
of a continuum rather than an isolated case. The quantitative answer is less
encouraging. The exact point achieves alignment $\EFourExactAlignment$, and the best
configuration anywhere in the family achieves $\EFourBestAlignment$ at distortion
$\EFourBestDistortion$, a gain of $\EFourAlignmentGain$. On these datasets the
trade-off exists but buys little, and we would not recommend paying for it.

\subsection{E5: noise and finite shots}
\label{subsec:e5}

\begin{figure}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.78\linewidth]{figures/e5_noise}
  \caption{E5. (a) The violation magnitude decays as $O(1/\sqrt{\text{shots}})$,
    tracking the dotted reference. (b) The violation \emph{count} saturates instead,
    because a constant fraction of triples in an ultrametric is exactly tight.
    (c) Depolarising noise leaves the geometry intact but flattens the profile until
    resolution collapses.}
  \label{fig:e5-noise}
\end{figure}

\Cref{prop:depolarising} predicts that depolarising noise cannot break the strong
triangle inequality, and the measurement agrees exactly: at every rate up to $0.99$ the
noiseless-estimate row has zero violations and zero excess. Resolution degrades, and
only at extreme rates.

Finite sampling is the real constraint, and measuring it correctly required abandoning
the obvious statistic. The violation count \emph{increases} with the shot budget towards
a plateau, which looks like a bug and is not: $\TieFraction$ of triples here meet
\cref{eq:strong-triangle} with equality, and any perturbation flips about half of them,
so the count converges to the tie fraction rather than to zero. The magnitude behaves as
expected, falling from $\ExcessAtLowShots$ at $\ExcessAtLowShotsCount$ shots to
$\ExcessAtHighShots$ at $\ExcessAtHighShotsCount$, a factor of $\sqrt{10}$ per decade.
Defining the budget on the magnitude gives \textbf{$\ShotBudget$ shots per kernel entry}
for a worst-case excess below $\ShotTargetExcess$, over $\ENoiseRepeats$ repetitions.
