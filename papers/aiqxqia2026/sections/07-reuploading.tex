\section{Beyond Exactness: Re-uploading and Noise}
\label{sec:reuploading}

\subsection{Why an extension must re-upload the data}

\Cref{thm:path-states} gives a fixed encoding with no trainable parameters. The obvious
way to add expressivity is to follow it with a parametrised circuit, but that does
nothing at all: for any unitary $W(\theta)$,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:unitary-invariance}
  \bigl\lvert \bra{\pathstate{x}} W(\theta)^{\dagger} W(\theta) \ket{\pathstate{y}}
  \bigr\rvert^{2} = \fid{\pathstate{x}}{\pathstate{y}} ,
\end{equation}
so the kernel is invariant. Any non-trivial extension must therefore re-upload the data
between layers \citep{PerezSalinasEtAl2020DataReuploading,
RodriguezGrasaEtAl2024NeuralQuantumKernels}. We use the family
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:reupload}
  \Phi^{(L)}(x) = W_L\, D_{\gamma}(x) \cdots W_1\, D_{\gamma}(x)\, \pathstate{x} ,
\end{equation}
where $D_{\gamma}(x)$ applies the phase $e^{i\gamma}$ to every node on the root-to-leaf
path of $x$ --- the natural data-dependent diagonal on the path-state Hilbert space ---
and each $W_\ell$ interpolates between the identity and a Haar-random unitary by a
parameter we call the entangling strength. At $\gamma = 0$ with trivial mixers,
\cref{eq:reupload} reduces exactly to $\pathstate{x}$, so the exact construction sits
at the origin of the family and anchors any trade-off curve at zero distortion.

We measure two quantities: the \emph{ultrametric distortion}
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:distortion}
  \mathcal{D} = \max_{x,y,z} \max\bigl(0,\; d(x,z) - \max(d(x,y), d(y,z))\bigr),
  \qquad d = 1 - K ,
\end{equation}
which vanishes exactly when the induced distance is an ultrametric, and the
kernel-target alignment as a standard proxy for usable expressivity.
\Cref{sec:experiments} maps the resulting Pareto front.

\subsection{Depolarising noise cannot break ultrametricity}

Exactness is a statement about an ideal kernel, so it is fair to ask what survives on
hardware. For the dominant global error channel the answer is: all of it.

\begin{proposition}[Depolarising noise preserves ultrametricity]
  \label{prop:depolarising}
  Let $K$ be ultrametric with non-decreasing profile $f$ on states of dimension $D$,
  and let $K_r = (1-r) K + r/D$ be the kernel estimated under global depolarising noise
  of rate $r \in [0,1]$. Then $d_r = 1 - K_r$ satisfies \cref{eq:strong-triangle} for
  every $r$, and $K_r$ is ultrametric with profile $(1-r) f + r/D$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The compute-uncompute estimator reads off the probability of the all-zeros outcome;
  replacing the state by the maximally mixed state with probability $r$ sends that
  probability to $1/D$, which gives the stated $K_r$. Hence
  \begin{equation*}
    d_r = 1 - (1-r)K - \frac{r}{D} = (1-r)\,d + r\Bigl(1 - \frac{1}{D}\Bigr),
  \end{equation*}
  an affine function $\varphi(t) = (1-r)t + c$ of $d$ with non-negative slope. An
  increasing affine map preserves order and commutes with $\max$, so
  $d(x,z) \le \max(d(x,y), d(y,z))$ implies
  $\varphi(d(x,z)) \le \max(\varphi(d(x,y)), \varphi(d(y,z)))$, which is
  \cref{eq:strong-triangle} for $d_r$. Applying $\varphi$ to $K = f(\lcadepth)$ gives
  the stated profile, which is still non-decreasing.
\end{proof}

What depolarising noise destroys is not geometry but contrast. The profile is
compressed towards the constant $1/D$, so consecutive levels become harder to tell
apart and the resolution depth of \Cref{def:diagnostics} falls, even though level
constancy stays exactly zero. \Cref{sec:experiments} measures where that collapse
happens.

Sampling noise behaves in the opposite way, and the reason is specific to ultrametric
spaces. Because every triangle in an ultrametric is isoceles with its two longest sides
equal, a constant fraction of triples --- $\TieFraction$ of them on the tree used in
our noise experiment --- satisfies \cref{eq:strong-triangle} \emph{with equality}. An
arbitrarily small independent perturbation flips about half of those into violations.
Counting violations is therefore an unstable statistic at low noise: it saturates
rather than decaying as the shot budget grows. The violation \emph{magnitude} is the
meaningful quantity, and it decays as $O(1/\sqrt{\text{shots}})$ as one would expect.
We return to this in \Cref{subsec:e5}.
