\title{Ultrametric Quantum Kernels: Exact $p$-adic Feature Maps for Hierarchical Data}

\author[1]{Paolo Giacomelli}[
  email=pgiacome@gmail.com,
]
\address[1]{Tenax S.p.A.}

\begin{abstract}
  A quantum kernel compares data through the fidelity of an encoding, so the geometry
  the model can express is fixed before any training happens. Hierarchical data
  (taxonomies, ontologies, phylogenies) is not Euclidean but ultrametric, and we ask
  which quantum feature maps induce an exactly ultrametric kernel. We give two
  obstructions and a matching construction. First, once the tree has depth at least
  two, no product feature map induces a \emph{strictly monotone} one, whatever its
  local dimensions: multiplicativity across digits limits any such encoding to a
  bounded resolution depth. Angle encoding is a corollary; computational-basis
  encoding is excluded separately, and is instructive because it \emph{is}
  ultrametric while resolving nothing.
  Second, and independently, the Gram matrix of any exact realisation forces a
  dimension of at least $L^{2} / \sum_{x,y} K(x,y)$ on $L$ points; on the $p$-adic tree
  with a geometrically decaying profile of parameter $s > 1$ this exceeds $2^{n}$ for
  every $p \ge 3$ and all sufficiently deep trees, so no $n$-qubit pure-state encoding
  suffices there, including the entangling IQP and ZZ maps the first argument cannot
  reach. Third, \emph{path states}, superpositions over a leaf's
  root-to-leaf ancestor chain with amplitudes read off the target profile, realise
  precisely the strictly monotone ultrametric kernels, on arbitrary rooted trees, and on
  $\Zpn$ they do so within an additive constant of that lower bound. We further show
  that global depolarising noise cannot break the strong triangle inequality at any
  rate, since it acts on distances as a non-decreasing affine map, and we measure the shot budget that finite
  sampling demands. Experiments on WordNet, Gene Ontology and the NCBI taxonomy confirm
  exactness to machine precision and separate ultrametricity from usable resolution: a
  basis encoding is ultrametric while resolving nothing. We claim no quantum speedup in
  kernel evaluation, and say so throughout; the contribution is a constraint on
  encoding design and an encoding, of optimal width on $\Zpn$, for models where no
  closed form survives.
\end{abstract}
